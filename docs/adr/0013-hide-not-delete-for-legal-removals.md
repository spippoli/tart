# Legal removals hide content; only a Purge by the Operator erases it

An Instance answers rights, privacy, and Digital Services Act requests by hiding content, not deleting it. Anyone can send a Notice from any public Archive record or Documentation item page, without an account. A Moderator closes it with a Withdrawal (a whole record or Documentation item), a Redaction (the content of past Revisions, when only a value must go, for example a person's real name in a description), or no action. Both actions are direct Moderator actions with their own log and no Revision, and a Reinstatement undoes them. Only a Purge, run from the Operator CLI on content that is already withdrawn or redacted, erases it permanently, and only when the law requires it (a founded GDPR erasure, an authority order). We chose this because the archive's value is that disappearance is never deletion, and moderation mistakes and abusive notices must be reversible, while GDPR erasure and orders sometimes demand real deletion. Keeping that irreversible step out of the Moderator UI makes it rare and deliberate.

## Considered Options

- **Withdrawal only**: correcting a value through an edit Submission leaves it visible in the public `/revisions` subpage, so a single unlawful value could only be hidden by withdrawing the whole record.
- **Deletion as the Moderator action**: irreversible, destroys archival evidence, and turns every abusive notice into permanent loss.

## Consequences

- A Notice captures the Art. 16(2) DSA elements: a reason from a configurable list (default: copyright in the depicted Artwork, copyright in the file, personal data, illegal content, other), the exact URL, an explanation, an optional name and email, and a good-faith statement. It gets an automatic acknowledgement and a decision notice. The notifier's name and email are erased six months after the decision, and the Notice log is kept without them.
- Every Withdrawal, Redaction, Reinstatement, and Submission rejection gives the affected Submitters a structured statement of reasons (Art. 17): the measure, the facts, notice or own initiative, the ground in the terms or law, whether automation was used (never, in the MVP), and redress. For a Documentation item the affected Submitter is the one who created it; for a record, its creator and the Submitters of the affected Revisions. Contesting is done by replying by email. The MVP has no in-app complaint system, which small Instances are exempt from.
- Account-level GDPR requests go to the Operator's privacy contact, not through Notices. Account deletion is self-service: email and credentials are erased, the display name becomes a stable pseudonym, drafts are deleted, open Submissions are retracted, approved content stays, and the User may choose to anonymise Creator credits that carry their name. Exports (Art. 15 and 20) are produced by the Operator through the CLI. The lawful basis for keeping deleted Users' contributions needs legal review.
- Moderators screen every Submission for identifiable people and number plates before approval, as *Russmedia* (C-492/23) requires for personal data. The MVP has no blurring tool: the Moderator requests changes, and the Submitter uploads a blurred file.
- Approving content into an authoritative archive may cost the operator the DSA Art. 6 hosting exemption for approved records, which needs legal review. Fast, granular Withdrawal and Redaction are the mitigation.
